# Kusama on JAM Prime: The Infrastructure Decision

*A decision paper on four infrastructure Wish-for-Change proposals and Kusama’s operating runway.*

13 October 2026 by Emiel Sebastiaan and Arief Ernst on behalf of SDFI BV.

**Status and purpose.** This decision paper follows *Kusama Futures in the JAM Era* [1] and *A Proposal for a Kusama Future on JAM Prime* [2]. It presents four concrete infrastructure Wish-for-Change proposals through which KSM DAO can select a complete security and resource endowment on JAM Prime. The selected proposal would establish Kusama’s political and financial mandate for agreement with DOT DAO, supported by an eligible Kusama Vision fund contribution and a Fellowship-led technical mandate.

**Appendix A | Kusama decision.** Four complete WFC resolutions, each including the common selection rules, directed to KSM holders and delegates.

**Appendix B | Polkadot acceptance.** The corresponding DOT-side resolution following selection of one Kusama proposal.

**Disclosures and acknowledgements.** The primary author leads a JAM implementation project, has business interests in staking and ventures researching or using JAM infrastructure, and has economic exposure to both ecosystems. The authors’ judgments are their own. Public discussion and technical conversations informed this work; governance support remains for the respective communities to determine. AI tools assisted research, drafting and editing, and the authors take responsibility for the paper. The authors thank participants who contributed evidence, criticism and technical assessment.

## Abstract

Kusama’s infrastructure decision forces a choice about its future strategy and offering. Infrastructure selection and strategy development must proceed in parallel: the intended offering determines resource needs, while available capacity, funding and runway determine what is achievable. The authors propose four infrastructure Wish-for-Change proposals: 16 cores for 2.5 years, 8 cores for 5 years, 4 cores for 10 years, or 2 cores for 20 years. Each seeks 40 coretime-years on JAM Prime, protected KSM control, adequate always-accumulate resources and Fellowship-led implementation and maintenance. Footprint planning ceilings are 450,000 JAMKB-equivalent for proposal 1, 350,000 for proposal 2 and 250,000 for proposals 3–4, with actual requirements assessed by the Fellowship.

The common reference is US$4,320,000, based on US$108,000 per CTY. The selected KSM referendum’s approval date fixes the DOT contribution using the preceding 90-day average USD price per DOT, within a ceiling of 5,000,000 DOT. Web3 Foundation (W3F) may confirm the eligible Kusama Vision fund contribution through explicit approval or explicit neutral abstention under the proposed arrangement. The remaining Kusama Vision fund resources support Kusama’s development through the Kusama Vision programme.

The selection procedure produces one mandate for DOT DAO consideration. The protected term begins when the complete agreed package is operational. Kusama would retire independent staking, retain authority over its monetary policy and renew its alignment with Polkadot. The authors advocate cohabitation and remain neutral among the four infrastructure WFC proposals.

## 1. From the position paper to a decision

### 1.1 The work so far

The scenario analysis examined Kusama’s infrastructure alternatives. The subsequent position paper developed a resource endowment, sovereignty requirements and an economic reference. This third paper converts that work and the ensuing discussion into a decision between four concrete infrastructure WFC proposals. [1] [2] [3]

JAM Prime denotes the Polkadot-aligned JAM instance contemplated as the shared infrastructure. Cohabitation combines that shared security and computation with a distinct KSM-governed domain. The endowment would provide specified resources and control rights for an agreed term. [2]

The earlier preferred proposal sought 16 cores for 5 years: 80 CTYs at a US$8,640,000 reference. The authors now propose 40 CTYs at US$4,320,000, retaining the US$108,000-per-CTY benchmark. Both references concern a complete package whose terms require counterparty acceptance. Appendix A develops the earlier paper’s template WFC into four standalone resolutions. [2]

### 1.2 A revised commitment of finite resources

The forum discussions and the authors’ conversations with stakeholders in Prague strengthened the case for reserving resources for both infrastructure and future development. [3] [9] A contribution of up to 5,000,000 DOT would use at most 50% of the original Kusama Vision fund commitment, leaving the remainder for existing obligations and continuing development. [5]

The four proposals establish their pricing rule in advance and let KSM holders choose the infrastructure scope and runway. It is the authors’ judgment that an offer of this order would be credible to DOT DAO. Formal support belongs to the respective governance processes.

### 1.3 The authors’ assessment

The authors regard cohabitation as the most economically credible infrastructure direction developed through this work. It combines shared security with KSM control and makes retirement of a separately financed validator system possible.

An alternate future with an **independent small-sized JAM** would still require Kusama to finance validators, bonded security capital, implementation and continuing maintenance. A smaller configuration reduces some expenditure while retaining a separate security economy and fixed engineering obligations. Its security and sustainable financing also require dedicated assessment. The authors have yet to see a funded design showing that these obligations fit Kusama’s means while preserving adequate resources for its offering.

**Continuing the relay chain with a smaller validator set** can reduce expenditure during transition. It preserves independent security and maintenance obligations and, as Polkadot moves towards JAM, requires a separate long-term technical mandate. It also leaves Kusama outside the opportunities enabled by JAM’s service model. The authors therefore regard validator downsizing as a useful interim measure, with the long-term infrastructure decision left open. These considerations follow the earlier scenario analysis. [1]

The discussion has produced alternatives and strategic ideas. In the authors’ assessment, it has yet to establish a sufficiently specified, funded and executable infrastructure alternative superior to cohabitation.

**The infrastructure decision forces a decision about Kusama’s strategy and offering.** Choosing capacity and runway requires the community to identify which functions and activities it intends to support. A sufficiently concrete strategy is necessary to establish infrastructure requirements and thereby decide which of the four proposals to prefer. The discussion has yet to provide that strategic clarity.

**Infrastructure selection and strategy development must proceed in parallel because they inform each other.** The emerging strategy specifies required capabilities and delivery time; the available infrastructure and remaining development budget establish what Kusama can afford to pursue. Both should be refined during the decision process.

## 2. Infrastructure and strategy: two decisions developed in parallel

### 2.1 The infrastructure decision

Kusama must choose the source, quantity and duration of its security and computation, the contribution it will make, the authority it retains and responsibility for technical stewardship. Each WFC combines these into one complete proposal.

Selection would establish a concrete basis for DOT DAO consideration and Fellowship work. It would also define the resources, protected term and infrastructure expenditure against which Kusama’s developing strategy must be assessed. That strategy should guide the choice of package during the same decision process.

Voters are being asked to accept the whole package attached to a proposal: the resource term, assessed footprint within its ceiling, protected authority, technical stewardship and payment formula. Supporting several acceptable proposals allows the common procedure to establish one preference. The selected result gives the Fellowship a defined problem to solve and gives DOT DAO a specified offer to assess. The community can reject every proposal if none meets its requirements.

### 2.2 The mission and strategy decision

The community and Kusama Vision programme must develop an offering that gives people reasons to use, build on and participate in Kusama. That work includes KSM’s economic role, required capabilities, funded activities and outcomes against which progress can be assessed.

WFC #498 emphasizes experimentation and advanced technologies; #573 advances a lightweight network direction. These starting points need translation into an executable Kusama Vision programme. The strategy should become sufficiently concrete during infrastructure selection to guide the choice of capacity and runway. It can continue to develop during implementation, informed by the opportunities and constraints of the selected package. Responsibility for its substance remains with Kusama’s community. [5] [4]

### 2.3 Why a direction is timely

The parachain service for JAM is still being designed. An indication of Kusama’s intended resources and retained functions can help the Fellowship account for them, particularly for the smaller proposals. The working launch expectation discussed with the authors is roughly 6 months from the paper’s publication date. [9]

A decision now gives the parties time to establish political support and organize technical work. The Fellowship should lead implementation. Mission and strategy development must proceed alongside this work so that technical requirements and strategic choices can be refined together.

### 2.4 The strategic consequences of the infrastructure choice

Greater simultaneous capacity supports a broader initial offering and brings renewal closer. A smaller allocation extends the protected term and requires a more concentrated offering. 16 cores for 2.5 years prioritize immediate scope; 2 cores for 20 years require a substantially smaller service arrangement. The strategy must identify which combination provides sufficient capacity and time to deliver the intended offering.

Each proposal preserves community authority over the activities it funds, its system upgrade path, its treasury, and the economic role of KSM. Voters should assess which starting conditions best support the work Kusama is prepared to undertake and finance during the term.

## 3. The infrastructure package and payment rule

### 3.1 The dollar reference and DOT ceiling

The common complete-package negotiating reference is **40 CTYs × US$108,000 per CTY = US$4,320,000**, retaining the earlier paper’s benchmark. [2]

The contribution from eligible Kusama Vision fund resources must remain within **5,000,000 DOT**. The selected WFC would establish political support for that limit. The calculated contribution may be lower, preserving more resources for development.

**Fixed DOT contribution = US$4,320,000 ÷ the preceding 90-day average USD price per DOT.**

Use CoinGecko’s 00:00 UTC snapshots for the 90 completed UTC days before the selected original KSM referendum’s approval date, excluding that date. Round the result up to the smallest transferable DOT unit. [8]

**The selected original KSM referendum’s approval date fixes the final DOT amount.** Each resolution in Appendix A includes the complete calculation rule.

### 3.2 Sufficiency and exchange-rate movements

The authors expect the ceiling to accommodate the reference despite price fluctuations before approval. 90-day averaging moderates the influence of 1 day’s price. The calculation fits within the ceiling when:

**90-day average ≥ US$0.864 per DOT** (US$4,320,000 ÷ 5,000,000 DOT).

At an illustrative US$1.00 average the contribution is 4,320,000 DOT; at US$0.90 it is 4,800,000 DOT. The actual average will follow the selected referendum’s approval date.

A calculation above 5,000,000 DOT requires revised terms and renewed KSM governance before payment. The resolution provides no automatic increase, truncation of the calculation, reduction of the package or substitution of another proposal.

DOT DAO would accept the fixed DOT amount for the complete package. Subsequent market movements leave those agreed obligations intact while changing the contribution’s USD value.

### 3.3 The Kusama Vision fund contribution and W3F’s participation

WFC #498 records W3F’s original **10,000,000 DOT** commitment for *the sole benefit of Kusama*. [5] Kusama Vision fund operators have confirmed that approximately **200,000 DOT** has currently been spent or earmarked for future spending. [11]

On that basis, approximately **9,800,000 DOT of the original commitment** remains before the infrastructure contribution. A contribution at the **5,000,000 DOT** ceiling would leave approximately **4,800,000 DOT** for the continuing Kusama Vision programme; a lower calculated contribution would preserve more. These planning balances deduct the reported expenditure and earmarked commitments once.

The authors propose that **W3F may confirm the eligible contribution through a support vote or a conscious neutral abstention recorded for this round**. Abstention permits W3F to remain neutral and leave selection to other KSM token holders. Ordinary nonparticipation provides no funding confirmation. Either recorded route would allow the selected community decision to guide the eligible contribution through the Kusama Vision fund’s administration.

### 3.4 Footprint, always-accumulate resources and protected KSM control

The authors propose footprint planning ceilings of **450,000 JAMKB-equivalent for infrastructure WFC proposal 1**, **350,000 for proposal 2** and **250,000 for proposals 3–4**. The Fellowship should assess actual requirements within the applicable ceiling.

The earlier study separated 250,000 JAMKB-equivalent for the 5-system-chain offering from 200,000 for initial support of other parachains. Proposal 1 retains the 450,000 ceiling. The authors use 350,000 for proposal 2 as an intermediate planning allowance, 100,000 above the earlier system reference. Proposals 3–4 seek a reduced or consolidated offering within 250,000. These are planning judgments for Fellowship assessment. [2]

Footprint depends on state, code, solicited PVF versions and migration requirements. Core count alone cannot establish the necessary amount. The Fellowship must assess the full operating package and any temporary needs within the selected ceiling; a finding requiring a material increase returns to governance. It must also quantify a sufficient allowance of always-accumulate resources and define a protected always-accumulate configuration.

The ceilings for proposals 1–2 preserve different allowances for the wider offering and temporary support. The 250,000 ceiling for proposals 3–4 directs their design towards a more concentrated inventory. The 350,000 figure is an intermediate request whose sufficiency remains for assessment. KSM governance retains internal allocation authority, while the Fellowship assesses retained functions and their dependencies. Each ceiling covers the complete assessed footprint needs.

Every proposal requires effective KSM control over internal allocation, authorization, service administration and upgrades. The implemented boundary must protect the resources and authority against ordinary unilateral revocation, reassignment or material impairment. The same standard applies at all 3 footprint ceilings, including restrictions on manager and supervisory powers and continuity through host upgrades.

### 3.5 The Fellowship mandate

On DOT DAO acceptance, the Fellowship would receive an accepted mandate for technical assessment, architecture, implementation, migration and maintenance within the complete package. It should organize delivery of a functioning Kusama domain, the agreed resources and protected control, continued Polkadot alignment and infrastructure maintenance throughout the term.

Detailed engineering belongs with the Fellowship. KSM governance retains material choices about the offering and monetary policy; departures from the selected political or financial terms require the relevant governance decision.

### 3.6 The complete package over time

Every proposal seeks the full resource, sovereignty and technical-stewardship package at the common dollar reference. The 450,000, 350,000 and 250,000 footprint ceilings specify the requested resource scope while retaining the common payment rule. Longer terms extend maintenance obligations; a smaller offering may reduce maintenance obligations. DOT DAO and the Fellowship must assess the complete commitment.

The protected term begins at verified operational availability of the complete agreed package. KSM governance should consider continuation sufficiently before expiry, informed by technical lead times.

## 4. The four infrastructure Wish-for-Change proposals

### 4.1 The comparison

All four proposals seek 40 CTYs, the same sovereignty standard and the same pricing method. Their capacity, duration and footprint planning ceilings define the infrastructure choice.

The earlier system baseline assigns 3 cores to Asset Hub and 1 each to Coretime, People, Bridge Hub and Encointer: 5 chains and 7 cores. The Fellowship must assess how that planning baseline maps to the final implementation. It leaves 9 cores within a 16-core estate and 1 within an 8-core estate before further reservations. [2]

| Infrastructure WFC proposal | Cores | Protected term | CTYs | Footprint ceiling, JAMKB-equivalent | Intended scope |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 16 | 2.5 years | 40 | 450,000 | 5 system chains and the greatest additional capacity |
| 2 | 8 | 5 years | 40 | 350,000 | 5 system chains with limited additional room |
| 3 | 4 | 10 years | 40 | 250,000 | Reduced or consolidated system services |
| 4 | 2 | 20 years | 40 | 250,000 | A substantially smaller service arrangement |

### 4.2 Infrastructure WFC proposal 1: 16 cores for 2.5 years

This proposal retains the intended 5-system-chain scope and provides the greatest room for additional workloads. KSM governance decides how to allocate capacity beyond the retained system functions.

Its 30-month term requires early work on both the offering and continuation. It suits voters prioritizing immediate scope and flexibility, with the Fellowship assessing delivery within the 450,000 JAMKB-equivalent ceiling.

### 4.3 Infrastructure WFC proposal 2: 8 cores for 5 years

This proposal retains the intended system scope over 5 years, leaving 1 core beyond the 7-core baseline. Additional workloads require disciplined choices. The 350,000 JAMKB-equivalent ceiling provides an intermediate footprint allowance, while execution remains limited to 8 cores.

### 4.4 Infrastructure WFC proposal 3: 4 cores for 10 years

This proposal provides 10 years of infrastructure for reduced or consolidated services within a 250,000 JAMKB-equivalent ceiling. An Asset Hub-centred arrangement is a possibility for Fellowship assessment. KSM governance decides which functions to retain, consolidate, access through shared services or discontinue.

### 4.5 Infrastructure WFC proposal 4: 2 cores for 20 years

This proposal supports a substantially smaller offering focused on functions KSM governance considers essential, within a 250,000 JAMKB-equivalent ceiling. An Asset Hub-centred arrangement is a possibility for Fellowship assessment. The Fellowship should assess achievable capabilities and performance. KSM governance decides which functions to retain, consolidate, access through shared services or discontinue.

### 4.6 Fellowship assessment and community choices

The Fellowship should determine how retained functions are implemented and resourced. Core count and footprint are separate constraints; neither alone establishes a functioning configuration. Consequential choices about Kusama’s offering arising from the selected infrastructure future return to KSM governance.

## 5. Consequences for KSM and the Kusama ecosystem

### 5.1 Security and the retirement of independent staking

Kusama would use JAM Prime’s security framework and retire its independent validator set, staking system and associated security issuance and rewards after the completed transition. The Fellowship should lead technical treatment of existing arrangements. All four proposals preserve the same standard of KSM control within shared infrastructure.

Ending KSM-funded validator remuneration changes Kusama’s economic obligations. The shared-infrastructure payment and accepted maintenance mandate provide the agreed infrastructure for the protected term. Activities outside that package still require funding. The continuing Kusama Vision programme therefore remains central to making the infrastructure useful and preparing the next operating period.

### 5.2 KSM as a strategic asset

KSM is Kusama’s greatest strategic asset: the basis of its political and economic domain. Retirement of independent security staking opens choices over issuance, supply and revenue treatment. KSM could become deflationary if burns exceed continuing issuance. Detailed monetary design remains an explicit KSM decision.

### 5.3 Non-system parachains and the future offering

The endowed estate substantially constrains the permissionless parachain offering. Proposals 1–2 retain some room beyond the system baseline; proposals 3–4 may leave little or none. Kusama’s coretime sales should be reduced in a timely manner to match the selected resource estate, with the Fellowship advising on implementation and existing commitments receiving their agreed treatment.

Parachain teams seeking continued hosting should consider migration to Polkadot under its applicable access terms. Affected teams may request a transition budget from the Kusama Treasury, subject to KSM governance approval. KSM governance should determine the activities supported within Kusama; additional resources require separately funded arrangements.

### 5.4 Continued alignment and shared primitives

Cohabitation should support **continued alignment between Kusama and Polkadot** on protocol evolution, technologies, standards and interfaces. Shared JAM Prime infrastructure provides a common technical foundation.

Kusama should benefit from maintained host code, Polkadot primitives, XCM, wallet support and developer tools. The Fellowship’s stewardship should preserve compatibility and assess relevant infrastructure changes. Shared engineering can reduce independent maintenance work while KSM governance continues to determine internal policy, allocation and the offering within its protected domain.

### 5.5 The continuing Kusama Vision programme

The remaining Kusama Vision fund resources should finance Kusama’s strategy and development after expenditure, commitments and the infrastructure contribution are accounted for. The Kusama Vision programme should connect a distinctive offering and KSM’s role to funded activities and assessable outcomes. Its strategic work should inform infrastructure selection and continue through implementation, with the chosen package establishing the resources and runway for delivery.

### 5.6 Opportunities enabled by JAM infrastructure

**JAM enables an offering beyond parachain hosting.** Its design supports programmable services using shared computation, state and security, with parachain hosting as 1 application of that infrastructure. Kusama could develop JAM-native services and workloads alongside retained parachain functions, broadening the technical possibilities available to its community. [10]

The authors see an opportunity to build a distinctive offering around those capabilities, including new applications, governance arrangements and economic uses of KSM. Continued alignment with Polkadot would connect that work to shared primitives, tools and services. The Kusama Vision programme should assess these opportunities as part of its strategy.

Realising them requires design, development funding and room within the selected compute, footprint and always-accumulate resources. The Fellowship should assess technical requirements and compatibility; KSM governance decides which activities to support. The four infrastructure proposals provide different starting conditions for that work. The opportunities enabled by JAM should therefore inform the capacity–runway choice alongside the requirements of existing services.

## 6. From four infrastructure WFC proposals to one mandate

### 6.1 Revising the existing direction

The selected proposal would supersede #573’s independent lightweight JAM direction and amend the use of Kusama Vision fund resources under #498 for this contribution. Existing commitments and the continuing Kusama Vision programme remain part of the funding reconciliation. The original Kusama Vision fund referendum already contemplated infrastructure support; the new decision specifies this expenditure. [4] [5]

### 6.2 A coordinated governance decision

Appendix A provides 4 self-contained resolutions with resource tables, 8 numbered clauses and an explanation of approval’s effect. Each includes its pricing and selection rules in full. The selected proposal establishes the mandate under those rules. Appendix B provides the corresponding Polkadot acceptance resolution.

Voters may support every acceptable proposal. After all four close, the passing proposal with the greatest final conviction-weighted AYE tally is selected. One passing proposal becomes the selection; none means no new mandate. An exact tie requires a further decision among the tied originals. Clause 8 of each resolution addresses interrupted rounds and unverifiable results.

WFCs are independent signals, so the common procedure defines how their outcomes produce one direction. Polkadot referendum #1710 supplies a precedent for supporting multiple acceptable proposals and comparing passing proposals’ conviction-weighted AYE totals. [6] [7]

### 6.3 One mandate and one price anchor

Selection establishes one political mandate to obtain the complete package at the calculated DOT contribution. The selected original referendum’s approval date remains the price anchor through certification and any tie resolution.

### 6.4 Effect of approval and implementation

The selected WFC records KSM support for the infrastructure direction, contribution and Fellowship delegation. Resource allocation, transfers and network changes proceed through appropriate authorized implementation actions. [6]

The Fellowship carries forward technical work under its accepted mandate. Material changes to resources, duration, footprint ceiling, protected rights, pricing, funding or the offering require the affected governance bodies’ decision. DOT DAO considers the complete selected package at its fixed DOT consideration.

## 8. The decision requested

The authors ask KSM DAO to select one infrastructure Wish-for-Change proposal for a complete endowment on JAM Prime. Each seeks 40 CTYs, protected KSM control, continued Polkadot alignment and Fellowship-led stewardship. The footprint planning ceiling is 450,000 JAMKB-equivalent for proposal 1, 350,000 for proposal 2 and 250,000 for proposals 3–4.

The selected original referendum’s approval date fixes the DOT offer from the US$4,320,000 reference and preceding 90-day average, within 5,000,000 DOT. W3F can support the vote or remain neutral through the proposed abstention route.

The authors advocate cohabitation and remain neutral among these four infrastructure WFC proposals. The infrastructure decision requires sufficient strategic clarity to choose the capacity and runway that Kusama needs. Infrastructure selection and strategy development should advance together, turning JAM’s opportunities into a funded offering within an agreed resource estate and protected operating term.
