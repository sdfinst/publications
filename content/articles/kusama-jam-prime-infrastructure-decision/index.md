# Kusama on JAM Prime: The Infrastructure Decision

*A decision paper on protected capacity, flexible coretime and Kusama’s operating runway.*

19 October 2026 by Emiel Sebastiaan and Arief Ernst on behalf of [SDFI BV](https://sdf.institute/).

**Status and purpose.** This decision paper follows *Kusama Futures in the JAM Era* [1] and *A Proposal for a Kusama Future on JAM Prime* [2]. It presents four infrastructure Wish-for-Change proposals through which KSM DAO can select a complete security and resource endowment on JAM Prime. The selected proposal would establish Kusama’s political and financial mandate for agreement with DOT DAO, supported by an eligible Kusama Vision fund contribution and a Fellowship-led technical mandate.

**Appendix A | Kusama decision.** Four complete WFC resolutions, each including its resource, pricing and selection rules, directed to KSM holders and delegates.

**Appendix B | Polkadot acceptance.** The corresponding DOT-side resolution following selection of one Kusama proposal.

**Disclosures and acknowledgements.** The primary author leads a JAM implementation project, has business interests in staking and ventures researching or using JAM infrastructure, and has economic exposure to both ecosystems. The authors’ judgments are their own. Public discussion and technical conversations informed this work; governance support remains for the respective communities to determine. AI tools assisted research, drafting and editing, and the authors take responsibility for the paper. The authors thank participants who contributed evidence, criticism and technical assessment.

## Abstract

Kusama’s infrastructure decision requires the community to identify the functions it wants to secure collectively, the duration of that protection and the contribution it is willing to make. Recent Parachain Service discussions indicate that Kusama parachains could use separately purchased JAM coretime while remaining within Kusama’s Parachain Service, subject to the final authorization and resource arrangements. This possibility allows a protected operating base to support a wider offering financed through additional resource purchases.

The authors propose four infrastructure Wish-for-Change proposals: **8 cores for 5 years, 6 cores for 6 years and 8 months, 4 cores for 10 years, or 2 cores for 20 years**. Each provides **40 coretime-years (CTYs)**. Every proposal seeks protected KSM control, sufficient always-accumulate resources and Fellowship-led implementation and maintenance. All four retain a **450,000 JAMKB-equivalent footprint planning ceiling**, comprising a **250,000 system base** and a **200,000 migration allowance**, with actual requirements assessed by the Fellowship.

Applying the existing **US$108,000-per-CTY benchmark** gives every proposal the same complete-package negotiating reference of **US$4,320,000**. The selected KSM referendum’s original approval date fixes its DOT contribution using the preceding 90-day average USD price per DOT, within a common ceiling of **5,000,000 DOT**. The remaining eligible Kusama Vision fund resources support existing commitments and the continuing Kusama Vision programme.

Infrastructure selection and strategy development must proceed in parallel. The guaranteed package establishes the protected base and runway; supplementary activity requires adequate funding, resources and support under its own arrangements. The authors therefore remove the earlier 16-core option and remain neutral among the four revised proposals. Selection produces one mandate for DOT DAO consideration. The protected term begins when the complete agreed package is operational.

## 1. From the position paper to a decision

### 1.1 The work so far

The scenario analysis examined Kusama’s infrastructure alternatives. The position paper developed a resource endowment, sovereignty requirements and an economic reference. This third paper converts that work and the ensuing discussion into four concrete infrastructure WFC proposals. [1] [2] [3]

JAM Prime denotes the Polkadot-aligned JAM instance contemplated as the shared infrastructure. Cohabitation combines shared security and computation with a distinct KSM-governed domain. The endowment would provide specified resources and effective control rights for an agreed term. [2]

The earlier position paper proposed an 80-CTY endowment at a US$8,640,000 reference. The revised proposals each seek 40 CTYs, retaining the US$108,000-per-CTY benchmark and establishing a common US$4,320,000 reference. This reference concerns a complete resource, sovereignty and technical-stewardship package whose terms require counterparty acceptance. Appendix A develops the earlier paper’s WFC template into four standalone resolutions.

### 1.2 A revised commitment of finite resources

The forum discussions and the authors’ conversations with stakeholders in Prague strengthened the case for reserving resources for both infrastructure and future development. [3] [9] A contribution of up to 5,000,000 DOT would use at most 50% of the original Kusama Vision fund commitment, leaving the remainder for existing obligations and continuing development. [5]

The four proposals establish a common 40-CTY endowment and pricing method in advance. KSM holders choose how to distribute that compute over time: greater simultaneous capacity for a shorter term, or a smaller guaranteed allocation for a longer operating runway. Every proposal retains the same footprint ceiling and dollar reference.

The authors consider an offer of this order a credible basis for negotiation with DOT DAO. Acceptance of the selected package, funding arrangement and technical mandate remains with the respective parties.

### 1.3 Flexible coretime and a protected operating base

JAM’s resource model creates a possible separation between Kusama’s guaranteed allocation and the total capacity used by its ecosystem. The Parachain Service design distinguishes coretime allocation from the service executing authorized work. [12] Technical discussions indicate that coretime acquired through Kusama could be used to run Polkadot parachains, and vice versa. This would allow a parachain to remain within Kusama’s Parachain Service while using JAM coretime acquired through Polkadot Coretime. [14] The final authorization rules, access arrangements and supporting resources require Fellowship assessment.

This would allow Kusama to secure an agreed operating base for a protected term while additional activity obtains resources through separate purchases. A Kusama parachain operator might acquire suitable coretime directly. The choice of an endowed core count would establish the capacity guaranteed by the bilateral agreement.

The same flexibility raises a question about the use of Kusama’s endowed capacity. The authorization discussion has identified that eligible work may target another JAM service when the authorizer does not restrict that destination. KSM governance should determine which allocations are reserved for system functions and the conditions under which other capacity is made available. The Fellowship should establish how those decisions can be enforced. [13]

Supplementary coretime must be accompanied by sufficient footprint. Use of available footprint within the agreed package remains subject to the system base, migration requirements and KSM allocation policy. Requirements beyond that package need separate provision. Additional purchases carry their own prices, access conditions and duration; the bilateral endowment defines the resources and protection guaranteed for Kusama’s operating base.

### 1.4 The authors’ assessment

The authors regard cohabitation as the most economically credible infrastructure direction developed through this work. It combines shared security with protected KSM control, permits retirement of a separately financed validator system and connects Kusama’s offering to JAM’s service model.

**Flexible access to additional coretime strengthens the case for concentrating the collective endowment on a viable operating base.** Kusama can assess which functions it wants to secure throughout the protected term and which activities could finance additional resources. On this basis, the authors no longer consider the earlier 16-core option warranted for this decision. The revised proposals offer 8, 6, 4 or 2 guaranteed cores, distributing the same 40 CTYs across protected terms of 5 years, 6 years and 8 months, 10 years or 20 years.

An **independent small-sized JAM** would continue to require Kusama to finance validators, bonded security capital, implementation and continuing maintenance. A smaller configuration reduces some expenditure while retaining a separate security economy and fixed engineering obligations. The authors have yet to see a sufficiently specified and funded design demonstrating that these obligations fit Kusama’s means while preserving adequate resources for its offering. This remains the authors’ assessment of the alternatives discussed. [1] [3]

**Continuing the relay chain with a smaller validator set** can reduce expenditure during transition. It retains independent security and maintenance obligations and would require a continuing technical mandate as Polkadot advances towards JAM. The authors regard validator downsizing as a useful interim measure while the long-term infrastructure decision is resolved. [1]

The smaller cohabitation proposals require a functioning base within their guaranteed resources. Flexible purchases offer a possible route for additional activity; the Fellowship must still establish which system functions and performance levels can be sustained within 6, 4 or 2 cores.

**The infrastructure decision forces a decision about Kusama’s strategy and offering.** The strategy must identify the functions to secure collectively, the activities expected to finance their own resource use and any further public support. That clarity is necessary to decide which of the four proposals to prefer.

**Infrastructure selection and strategy development must proceed in parallel because they inform each other.** Strategy specifies required capabilities and delivery time. Guaranteed resources, supplementary resource costs and the remaining development budget establish what Kusama can afford to pursue. The authors advocate cohabitation and remain neutral among the four revised infrastructure WFC proposals.

## 2. Infrastructure and strategy: two decisions developed in parallel

### 2.1 The infrastructure decision

Kusama must choose the source, quantity and duration of its guaranteed security and computation, the contribution it will make, the authority it retains and responsibility for technical stewardship. Each WFC combines these into one complete package.

Selection would establish a concrete basis for DOT DAO consideration and Fellowship work. It would define the protected operating base and the infrastructure expenditure against which Kusama’s strategy must be assessed.

Voters are being asked to accept the whole package: the resource term, assessed footprint within its ceiling, protected authority, technical stewardship and pricing rule. Supporting several acceptable proposals allows the common procedure to establish one preference. The selected result gives the Fellowship a defined problem to solve and DOT DAO a specified offer to assess. The community can reject every proposal if none meets its requirements.

### 2.2 The mission and strategy decision

The community and Kusama Vision programme must develop an offering that gives people reasons to use, build on and participate in Kusama. That work includes KSM’s economic role, required capabilities, funded activities and outcomes against which progress can be assessed.

WFC #498 emphasizes experimentation and advanced technologies; #573 establishes an independent lightweight JAM direction. These starting points require translation into an executable programme. The strategy should become sufficiently concrete during infrastructure selection to guide the choice of guaranteed capacity, runway and contribution. [5] [4]

A wider offering may combine activity supported by the endowment with activity financed by participants or separately authorized public funding. The strategy should identify the expected resource costs and responsibilities in each case. Its substance remains with Kusama’s community and can continue to develop during implementation.

### 2.3 Why a direction is timely

The Parachain Service for JAM remains under development. An indication of Kusama’s intended resources, retained functions and supplementary-capacity requirements can help the Fellowship account for them, particularly for the smaller proposals. [12]

The working launch expectation reported to the authors in October 2026 was roughly 6 months from that period. It is a planning assumption from those discussions; implementation readiness remains for the technical teams to establish. [9]

A decision gives the parties time to establish political support and organize technical work. The Fellowship should lead implementation. Mission and strategy development should proceed alongside it so that technical requirements and strategic choices can be refined together.

### 2.4 The strategic consequences of the infrastructure choice

Greater endowed capacity supports more activity within the protected package. A smaller allocation concentrates that guaranteed base and allows the community to choose a longer operating term. A wider offering may use separately financed resources where technically supported.

All four options commit the same 40 CTYs at the same dollar reference. Voters must assess the balance between simultaneous capacity and duration, the operating base each allocation can sustain, and the extent to which further activity would depend on additional purchases.

Each proposal preserves community authority over the activities it funds, its system upgrade path, its treasury and the economic role of KSM. The strategy should identify which starting conditions best support the work Kusama is prepared to undertake and finance.

## 3. The infrastructure package and payment rule

### 3.1 The common dollar reference and DOT ceiling

The authors retain the earlier paper’s **US$108,000-per-CTY benchmark**. Each proposed allocation comprises **40 CTYs**, giving the same complete-package negotiating reference of **US$4,320,000**. The protected term is calculated as **40 CTYs divided by the number of guaranteed cores**. [2]

| Infrastructure WFC proposal | Guaranteed cores | Protected term | CTYs | Complete-package reference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 8 | 5 years | 40 | US$4,320,000 |
| 2 | 6 | 6 years and 8 months | 40 | US$4,320,000 |
| 3 | 4 | 10 years | 40 | US$4,320,000 |
| 4 | 2 | 20 years | 40 | US$4,320,000 |

The contribution from eligible Kusama Vision fund resources must remain within **5,000,000 DOT**. A lower calculated contribution preserves more resources for development.

**Fixed DOT contribution = the selected proposal’s dollar reference ÷ the preceding 90-day average USD price per DOT.**

Use CoinGecko’s 00:00 UTC snapshots for the 90 completed UTC days before the selected original KSM referendum’s approval date, excluding that date. The average is arithmetic. Round the contribution upward to the smallest transferable DOT unit. [8]

**The selected original KSM referendum’s approval date fixes its DOT amount.** Appendix A specifies the date, calculation and safeguards in each standalone resolution. The result record should retain the approval block, dated price observations, arithmetic mean and final rounding calculation.

### 3.2 Sufficiency and exchange-rate movements

All four proposals share the **US$4,320,000** reference and **5,000,000 DOT** ceiling. The calculated contribution fits the ceiling when the applicable 90-day average is at least **US$0.864 per DOT**.

At an illustrative US$1.00 average, any of the proposals would require **4,320,000 DOT**. At US$1.50, the contribution would be **2,880,000 DOT**. An average below US$0.864 would produce a contribution above the ceiling.

A calculation above 5,000,000 DOT requires revised terms and renewed KSM governance before payment. The resolution provides no automatic increase, truncation of the calculated contribution, reduction of the package or substitution of another proposal.

DOT DAO would accept the selected fixed DOT amount for the complete package. Subsequent market movements leave those agreed obligations intact while changing the contribution’s USD value.

### 3.3 The Kusama Vision fund contribution and W3F’s participation

WFC #498 records W3F’s original **10,000,000 DOT** commitment for the sole benefit of Kusama, administered through a programme of deployment. The proposed infrastructure contribution would use eligible resources from that commitment. [5]

The operators’ confirmation supplied to the authors identified approximately **200,000 DOT** as spent or earmarked for future spending. [11] On that reported basis, approximately **9,800,000 DOT of the original commitment** remained before an infrastructure contribution. A contribution at the **5,000,000 DOT** ceiling would leave approximately **4,800,000 DOT** for the continuing Kusama Vision programme; a lower contribution would preserve more. Updated expenditure and outstanding obligations must be reconciled before payment, with each commitment deducted once.

The authors propose that **W3F may confirm the eligible contribution through a support vote or a conscious neutral abstention recorded for this round**. A recorded neutral route would allow W3F to leave selection to other KSM holders. Ordinary nonparticipation provides no funding confirmation. This proposed arrangement requires W3F’s acceptance and the fund’s applicable administration.

The selected community decision would guide the eligible contribution. Existing commitments and the remaining resources for development remain part of the reconciliation.

### 3.4 Footprint, always-accumulate resources and protected KSM control

All four infrastructure WFC proposals retain a **450,000 JAMKB-equivalent total footprint planning ceiling**, divided into a **250,000 system base** and a **200,000 migration allowance**. The split follows the technical study’s footprint model. [2]

| Footprint component | Planning allocation, JAMKB-equivalent | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **System base** | **250,000** | The reference configuration of 5 system chains, including host-side state, the shared host and authorizer programs, 1 active and 2 additional solicited PVF versions per system chain, the shared host’s 3-version allowance, and contingency reserves. |
| **Migration allowance** | **200,000** | Initial footprint backing for the 12 other parachains in the study’s inventory, allowing 1 PVF version and generic host-side state per chain, plus contingency reserves. |
| **Total footprint ceiling** | **450,000** | The combined system base and migration allowance, subject to Fellowship assessment of actual requirements. |

The system-base calculation uses Asset Hub, Coretime, People, Bridge Hub and Encointer. The Fellowship should assess the system base and initial migration needs separately against the functions retained in each configuration and the actual implementation. Footprint principally follows registered parachains, retained program versions and admitted state, so different compute allocations can retain the same footprint planning request. Execution capacity remains governed by the selected core allocation and any separately acquired coretime. [2]

Both components form part of the protected footprint package for the agreed term. KSM governance determines participant quotas, backing for future upgrades and permissible reallocation. Releasing a parachain’s transitional backing requires replacement backing or removal of the dependent state. Overlapping upgrades and state growth require additional backing before they exceed the participant’s quota. A material increase in the total ceiling returns to governance. [2]

The Fellowship must quantify sufficient always-accumulate resources and define their protected configuration. It should establish how any supplementary workloads use available allowances or obtain additional resources while preserving the operating base.

Every proposal requires effective KSM control over internal allocation, authorization, service administration and upgrades. The implemented boundary must protect the agreed resources and authority against ordinary unilateral revocation, reassignment or material impairment. The same standard applies to all four proposals, including restrictions on manager and supervisory powers and continuity through host upgrades.

KSM governance determines the permitted uses of endowed capacity, including reservations for system functions and the terms on which capacity is made available to other users. Those decisions must be enforceable through the implemented authorization and assignment arrangements.

### 3.5 The Fellowship mandate

On DOT DAO acceptance, the Fellowship would receive an accepted mandate for technical assessment, architecture, implementation, migration and maintenance within the complete package. It should organize delivery of a functioning Kusama domain, the agreed resources and protected control, continued Polkadot alignment and infrastructure maintenance throughout the term.

The assessment should identify an operating base and migration arrangement that fit the guaranteed package, assessing the system base and migration allowance separately. It should also establish whether parachains can use separately acquired JAM coretime while remaining within Kusama’s Parachain Service. This includes access and authorization, resource accounting, supporting footprint and always-accumulate requirements, and the scope of technical support.

The Fellowship should assess how KSM allocation policy protects reserved resources and how supplementary activity can be accommodated without impairing the agreed base. Direct purchases and any intermediary arrangements can be considered within that assessment. Further resource or support commitments require their own agreement and funding.

Detailed engineering belongs with the Fellowship. KSM governance retains material choices about the offering and monetary policy; departures from the selected political or financial terms require the relevant governance decision.

### 3.6 The complete package over time

Every proposal seeks a complete resource, sovereignty and technical-stewardship package at its stated dollar reference. The common benchmark provides a negotiating basis. DOT DAO and the Fellowship must assess and accept the entire obligation, including implementation and maintenance over the selected term.

Longer terms extend the duration of those obligations, while a smaller operating base may reduce some work. The 20-year proposal therefore requires an explicit commitment to continuity through technical change.

The protected term begins at verified operational availability of the complete agreed package. KSM governance should consider continuation sufficiently before expiry, informed by technical lead times and the strategy’s progress.

## 4. The four infrastructure Wish-for-Change proposals

### 4.1 The comparison

The four proposals share a 40-CTY endowment, US$4,320,000 reference, sovereignty standard, pricing method and DOT ceiling. Their common footprint package comprises a 250,000 JAMKB-equivalent system base and a 200,000 JAMKB-equivalent migration allowance, totaling 450,000 JAMKB-equivalent. Their guaranteed simultaneous capacity and protected duration define the infrastructure choice.

The earlier system planning baseline assigns 3 cores to Asset Hub and 1 each to Coretime, People, Bridge Hub and Encointer: 5 chains and 7 cores. The Fellowship must assess how that baseline maps to the final implementation. It fits arithmetically within 8 cores, leaving 1 before further reservations. Allocations of 6, 4 or 2 cores require an adapted operating arrangement. [2]

The authors have presented these configurations to Fellowship members through direct correspondence and through the Parachain Service AMA. No technical objection to pursuing them has been raised in those exchanges. The Fellowship’s assessment should establish the service arrangements, retained functions and performance achievable within each allocation. [14]

The intended scopes below describe the offering supported by the endowment. Supplementary activity depends on separately secured resources and suitable technical arrangements.

| Infrastructure WFC proposal | Guaranteed cores | Protected term | CTYs | System base + migration allowance, JAMKB-equivalent | Intended scope within the endowment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 8 | 5 years | 40 | 250,000 + 200,000 | Intended 5-system-chain scope with limited additional endowed capacity |
| 2 | 6 | 6 years and 8 months | 40 | 250,000 + 200,000 | A system-focused base requiring adaptation of the 7-core planning configuration |
| 3 | 4 | 10 years | 40 | 250,000 + 200,000 | Reduced or consolidated system services |
| 4 | 2 | 20 years | 40 | 250,000 + 200,000 | A concentrated operating base focused on essential functions |

### 4.2 Infrastructure WFC proposal 1: 8 cores for 5 years

This proposal offers the greatest guaranteed simultaneous capacity. It seeks the intended 5-system-chain scope, leaving 1 core beyond the earlier 7-core baseline before further reservations. It retains the common system base and migration allowance.

KSM governance decides how to allocate capacity beyond retained system functions. The proposal secures 40 CTYs and provides 5 years to develop the offering and prepare continuation.

### 4.3 Infrastructure WFC proposal 2: 6 cores for 6 years and 8 months

This proposal provides 6 years and 8 months for an adapted system offering, supported by the common system base and migration allowance. The 6-core allocation falls below the earlier 7-core configuration. The Fellowship should assess resource sharing, changes in block production, consolidation and use of shared services.

The proposal preserves the common 40-CTY endowment while extending the protected term beyond the 5 years of the 8-core proposal.

### 4.4 Infrastructure WFC proposal 3: 4 cores for 10 years

This proposal provides 10 years for a reduced or consolidated offering, supported by the common system base and migration allowance. The Fellowship should assess resource sharing, changes in block production, consolidation and use of shared services.

The proposal secures 40 CTYs. It offers the same total compute-time commitment as proposal 1 across a longer term, with half its simultaneous guaranteed capacity.

### 4.5 Infrastructure WFC proposal 4: 2 cores for 20 years

This proposal provides the longest protected term for a concentrated operating base with the common system base and migration allowance. The Fellowship should assess the functions, service arrangement and performance achievable within 2 guaranteed cores.

The proposal provides 40 CTYs over 20 years, with one quarter of the 8-core proposal’s simultaneous guaranteed capacity. Its protected rights, resources and maintenance obligations continue throughout the term.

### 4.6 Fellowship assessment and community choices

The Fellowship should determine how retained functions are implemented and resourced. Core count and footprint are separate constraints; both must support a functioning configuration alongside sufficient always-accumulate resources.

For each proposal, the assessment should distinguish the operating base sustained by guaranteed resources from supplementary activity. It should make clear any additional purchase requirements, performance changes and implications for maintenance. Consequential choices about Kusama’s offering return to KSM governance.

## 5. Consequences for KSM and the Kusama ecosystem

### 5.1 Security and the retirement of independent staking

Kusama would use JAM Prime’s security framework and retire its independent validator set, staking system and associated security issuance and rewards after the completed transition. The Fellowship should lead technical treatment of existing arrangements. All four proposals preserve the same standard of KSM control within shared infrastructure.

Ending KSM-funded validator remuneration changes Kusama’s economic obligations. The agreed contribution and accepted maintenance mandate provide the complete infrastructure package for its protected term. The continuing Kusama Vision programme remains central to making the infrastructure useful and preparing the next operating period.

### 5.2 KSM as a strategic asset

The authors regard KSM as Kusama’s greatest strategic asset: the basis of its political and economic domain. Retirement of independent security staking opens choices over issuance, supply and revenue treatment. KSM could become deflationary if burns exceed continuing issuance. Detailed monetary design remains an explicit KSM decision.

The strategy should define KSM’s role in participation, fees and resource allocation, including activity that obtains coretime through the wider JAM market. Infrastructure access, a distinctive offering and KSM’s economic role should develop together.

### 5.3 Non-system parachains and the future offering

The endowed estate determines the resources available within the complete package. Proposal 1 retains limited capacity beyond the earlier system baseline; proposals 2–4 focus on an adapted operating base. KSM governance decides how endowed resources are allocated. Coretime commitments backed by that endowment must be aligned with the selected allocation, with Fellowship advice and existing commitments receiving their agreed treatment.

Non-system parachains may be able to continue within Kusama’s Parachain Service using separately funded JAM resources. The Fellowship should assess this route alongside other available migration options under their applicable access terms. Teams can then assess the technical requirements, costs and governance arrangements of the available options. [12] [14]

The migration allowance provides initial footprint backing for non-system parachains under defined quotas. Additional resource purchases must have sufficient supporting capacity and technical support. Ordinary purchases can proceed under the applicable access and allocation rules. New public expenditure or obligations beyond the agreed package require separate authorization. Affected teams may request a transition budget from the Kusama Treasury through a separate KSM funding decision.

### 5.4 Continued alignment and shared primitives

Cohabitation should support continued alignment between Kusama and Polkadot on protocol evolution, technologies, standards and interfaces. Shared JAM Prime infrastructure provides a common technical foundation.

Kusama should benefit from maintained host code, Polkadot primitives, XCM, wallet support and developer tools. The Fellowship’s stewardship should preserve compatibility and assess relevant infrastructure changes. Shared engineering can reduce independent maintenance work while KSM governance continues to determine policy, allocation and the offering within its protected domain.

### 5.5 The continuing Kusama Vision programme

The remaining Kusama Vision fund resources should finance Kusama’s strategy and development after expenditure, commitments and the infrastructure contribution are accounted for. The Kusama Vision programme should connect a distinctive offering and KSM’s role to funded activities and assessable outcomes.

Its strategic work should inform infrastructure selection and continue through implementation. The programme should identify which further activities require public support and which can finance their own resources.

### 5.6 Opportunities enabled by JAM infrastructure

JAM enables an offering beyond parachain hosting. Its design supports programmable services using shared computation, state and security, with parachain hosting as 1 application. Kusama could develop JAM-native services and workloads alongside retained parachain functions. [10]

The authors see an opportunity to develop new applications, governance arrangements and economic uses of KSM. Continued alignment with Polkadot would connect that work to shared primitives, tools and services. The Kusama Vision programme should assess these opportunities as part of its strategy.

Realising them requires design, development funding and sufficient compute, footprint and support. These may come from available resources within the package or separately funded provision. The Fellowship assesses technical requirements and compatibility; KSM governance decides which activities receive collective support.

## 6. From four infrastructure WFC proposals to one mandate

### 6.1 Revising the existing direction

The selected proposal would supersede WFC #573’s independent lightweight JAM direction and amend the use of Kusama Vision fund resources under WFC #498 for the infrastructure contribution. Existing commitments and the continuing Kusama Vision programme remain part of the funding reconciliation. The original Kusama Vision fund referendum already contemplated infrastructure support; this decision specifies a complete endowment and its contribution. [4] [5]

### 6.2 A coordinated governance decision

Appendix A provides four self-contained resolutions, each with a resource table, 8 numbered clauses and an explanation of approval’s effect. The coordinated round comprises 8 cores for 5 years, 6 cores for 6 years and 8 months, 4 cores for 10 years and 2 cores for 20 years. Each resolution includes the common dollar reference and the complete pricing and selection rules.

Voters may support every acceptable proposal. After all four close through approval or rejection, the passing proposal with the greatest exact final conviction-weighted AYE tally is selected. One passing proposal becomes the selection; none means no new mandate. An exact tie requires a further decision among the tied original approved proposals. Clause 8 addresses interrupted rounds and unverifiable results.

WFCs are independent governance signals. The proposed common procedure specifies how their outcomes establish one direction. Polkadot referendum #1710 provides a precedent for supporting multiple acceptable alternatives and comparing the conviction-weighted AYE totals of passing proposals. [6] [7]

### 6.3 One mandate and one price anchor

Selection establishes one political mandate to obtain the winning proposal’s complete package at its calculated DOT contribution. Its original approval date remains the price anchor through result certification and any tie resolution.

Each proposal uses the same dollar reference. Its original approval date determines the applicable 90-day average, so referenda approved on different dates can produce different DOT amounts. Only the selected proposal’s calculation becomes the offer advanced to DOT DAO. A ceiling breach requires renewed governance authority; the result does not automatically pass to another option.

### 6.4 Effect of approval and implementation

The selected WFC records KSM support for the infrastructure direction, contribution and Fellowship delegation. Resource allocation, transfers and network changes proceed through appropriate authorized implementation actions. [6]

The Fellowship carries forward technical work under its accepted mandate. Material changes to the guaranteed resources, term, footprint ceiling, protected rights, pricing, funding or agreed offering require the affected governance bodies’ decision. Supplementary purchases can proceed under their applicable rules; any enlarged public commitment requires separate authority. DOT DAO considers the complete selected package at its fixed DOT contribution.

## 8. The decision requested

The authors ask KSM DAO to select one infrastructure Wish-for-Change proposal for a complete endowment on JAM Prime: **8 cores for 5 years, 6 cores for 6 years and 8 months, 4 cores for 10 years, or 2 cores for 20 years**. Each seeks protected KSM control, continued Polkadot alignment and Fellowship-led stewardship. All four retain a 250,000 JAMKB-equivalent system base and a 200,000 JAMKB-equivalent migration allowance within the common 450,000 JAMKB-equivalent footprint planning ceiling.

Each proposal provides 40 CTYs at the common US$4,320,000 reference. The selected original referendum’s approval date fixes the DOT contribution using the preceding 90-day average, within 5,000,000 DOT. W3F may confirm eligible funding through the proposed support or recorded neutral-abstention route.

The authors advocate cohabitation and remain neutral among the four proposals. Flexible coretime strengthens the opportunity to combine a protected operating base with separately financed activity. Governance must decide which functions to guarantee, how long to protect them and the total contribution it is willing to commit. ## Appendix A. Four infrastructure Wish-for-Change proposals

### A.1 Infrastructure Wish-for-Change proposal 1 (8 cores for 5 years)

The earlier 7-core planning baseline provides for 5 system parachains, leaving up to 1 core for other workloads or reserves within this endowment. This proposal provides the greatest reserved capacity among the four options and 5 years of protected infrastructure. The Fellowship must assess the actual operating arrangement and footprint.

**Proposed resolution**

Subject to selection by the rules in this resolution, Kusama’s token-holder governance (KSM DAO) supports a complete security and resource endowment on **JAM Prime**, the Polkadot-aligned JAM instance contemplated as shared infrastructure, comprising **8 cores for 5 years**, totaling **40 coretime-years (CTYs)**, on these terms.

**1. Infrastructure direction.** KSM DAO pursues a protected Kusama-controlled service jurisdiction on JAM Prime, superseding WFC #573’s independent lightweight JAM direction upon selection (https://kusama.subsquare.io/referenda/573). Kusama’s community and the Kusama Vision programme retain responsibility for its mission and strategy.

**2. Specified resource package.** The following resources define the protected package and the operating scope it secures.

| Resource or term | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Compute | 8 full cores reserved throughout the term; 40 CTYs. |
| Protected term | 5 years from verified operational availability of the complete package. |
| System base footprint | **250,000 JAMKB-equivalent planning allowance** for system functions and shared host resources. |
| Migration footprint allowance | **200,000 JAMKB-equivalent planning allowance** for initial support of 12 other parachains. |
| Total footprint planning ceiling | **450,000 JAMKB-equivalent**, backed and usable throughout the protected term; actual requirements assessed by the Fellowship. |
| Always-accumulate resources | Protected always-accumulate registration and a sufficient quantified minimum allowance, assessed by the Fellowship. |
| Control | Protected KSM authority throughout the term. |
| Stewardship | Accepted, Fellowship-led implementation and maintenance of the agreed operating scope. |
| Consideration | Fixed DOT amount under clause 5 for the complete package. |

The system base uses the planning model for 5 system chains (Asset Hub, Coretime, People, Bridge Hub and Encointer), shared host state and programs, 2 reusable authorizer-program blobs, 1 active and 2 additional solicited Parachain Validation Function (PVF) versions per chain, 3 host-program versions and contingency. The migration allowance provides initial support for 12 other parachains, with 1 PVF version and generic host-side state per chain plus contingency.

KSM governance must define participant quotas, future upgrade backing and permissible reallocation. Participants must secure additional backing before state growth or overlapping upgrades exceed their quotas. Transitional backing may be released only once replacement backing is in place or the dependent state has been removed. The complete footprint entitlement remains protected throughout the agreed term.

KSM governance may permit additional Kusama activity using separately funded JAM coretime where the implemented service supports it. Additional purchases follow their own availability, pricing, duration and protection terms. Their footprint, always-accumulate resources and maintenance needs must fit assessed available capacity or receive additional funded provision. Such purchases do not enlarge this endowment or automatically acquire its resource protections, protected term or stewardship commitments.

**3. Protected KSM control.** KSM governance retains authority over service upgrades, internal governance, monetary policy, authorizers and internal resource assignment. It determines reservations for retained functions and the permitted uses and conditions for releasing other endowed capacity. The implemented boundary must prevent ordinary unilateral revocation, reassignment or material impairment through manager and other host administrative powers. Protection covers the agreed compute, effective footprint backing, always-accumulate resources and KSM authority through host upgrades throughout the term.

**4. Fellowship mandate and continued alignment.** KSM DAO delegates technical assessment, architecture, implementation, migration and maintenance to the Polkadot Fellowship under an accepted mandate. The Fellowship should assess the system base and initial migration needs separately and establish a viable arrangement for the agreed offering within the reserved compute, assessed footprint and sufficient always-accumulate resources.

The assessment should cover use of externally acquired JAM coretime by the Kusama service, including possible direct purchases by users, the associated authorization and operating requirements, and controls over endowed capacity released for use elsewhere. Material changes to the agreed package, including necessary footprint ceiling revisions, return to the relevant governance bodies. KSM governance retains material offering decisions. The mandate preserves Kusama–Polkadot alignment through shared technologies, primitives, standards and interfaces.

**5. Complete-package consideration and DOT pricing.** The entire package’s reference is **US$4,320,000 = 40 CTYs × US$108,000 per CTY**, with a contribution ceiling of **5,000,000 DOT**. **D** is the UTC calendar date of the finalized block recording this individual Kusama referendum’s approval. **P90(D)** is the arithmetic mean of CoinGecko’s **00:00 UTC USD price snapshots for 1 DOT**, asset identifier **polkadot**, for all 90 completed UTC days **D−90 through D−1**, inclusive.

**Fixed DOT consideration = US$4,320,000 ÷ P90(D), rounded upward to the smallest transferable DOT unit.**

The original approval date remains the pricing anchor through result certification, tie-breaking, DOT DAO voting and settlement. Its calculation fixes the complete-package offer. Once accepted by DOT DAO, that DOT amount remains unchanged despite market movements; its future USD value remains variable.

A result above 5,000,000 DOT requires revised terms and renewed KSM governance before payment. This resolution provides no automatic ceiling increase, truncation of the calculated contribution, reduction of the package or substitution of another proposal.

**6. Kusama Vision fund and Kusama Vision programme.** The contribution uses eligible Kusama Vision fund resources, amending WFC #498 while preserving the Kusama Vision programme and existing commitments (https://kusama.subsquare.io/referenda/498). Expenditure and outstanding obligations are reconciled; the remainder supports development.

This proposed arrangement is subject to acceptance by Web3 Foundation (W3F). W3F may confirm the eligible contribution through an explicit support vote or a conscious neutral abstention explicitly recorded for this round, allowing other KSM holders to decide. Ordinary nonparticipation provides no funding confirmation.

**7. Consequences for Kusama.** The independent validator set, staking system and associated staking issuance and rewards retire with the completed transition. Further monetary design requires KSM decisions; deflation remains possible. KSM governance retains offering decisions and treatment of existing commitments.

Coretime sales and other commitments backed by the endowment must fit its available capacity after reservations for retained functions. Parachain teams seeking capacity beyond that allocation may pursue continued hosting within the Kusama service using separately funded resources where technically supported, or migration to Polkadot under its applicable access terms. Affected teams may request a Kusama Treasury transition budget through a separate KSM funding decision.

**8. Selection and bilateral acceptance.** The coordinated round consists of four infrastructure WFC proposals: 8 cores for 5 years, 6 cores for 6 years and 8 months, 4 cores for 10 years and 2 cores for 20 years. Voters may support every acceptable proposal. Each must independently satisfy the applicable approval and support thresholds.

After all four close through approval or rejection, the passing proposal with the greatest exact final conviction-weighted AYE tally is selected. Early approval fixes its tally and pricing date while selection awaits the remaining outcomes.

Cancellation, killing or timeout requires a fresh governance decision for the incomplete round. Unverifiable tallies leave selection unresolved. No passing proposal means no mandate. Exact ties among the highest passing tallies require a further decision among those original approved proposals, preserving the winner’s original approval date.

Only the winner advances to Polkadot’s token-holder governance (DOT DAO) for acceptance of the complete package and fixed consideration: **One mandate and one contribution**.

**Effect of this vote.** Approval establishes a political direction, financial rule and requested technical mandate, conditional on selection. Resource allocation, payment and technical changes require authorized implementation actions; the WFC itself transfers no funds and grants no technical privileges.

**1. Acceptance of the Kusama outcome.** DOT DAO accepts the complete package identified above, selected through the concluded Kusama voting round. Its core count, protected term, CTY total, footprint ceiling and component allowances carry forward unchanged.

**2. Acceptance of the fixed consideration.** DOT DAO accepts the stated DOT amount as payment for the complete package. It is calculated from the stated dollar reference divided by the arithmetic mean of CoinGecko’s 00:00 UTC USD price snapshots for 1 DOT, asset identifier polkadot, for the 90 completed UTC days preceding the selected original Kusama referendum’s approval date, excluding that date, and rounded upward to the smallest transferable DOT unit.

The selected original approval date remains the pricing anchor through result certification, tie-breaking, this vote and settlement. Acceptance, settlement and subsequent market movements leave the agreed DOT amount unchanged. Any calculated amount above 5,000,000 DOT requires revised terms and renewed KSM governance before payment. This resolution provides no automatic ceiling increase, truncation of the contribution, reduction of the package or substitution of another proposal.

**3. Resources and protected control.** The package includes the reserved compute, Fellowship-assessed footprint within the stated ceiling, and sufficient quantified always-accumulate resources with protected registration. KSM governance retains authority over service administration, upgrades, authorizers, internal allocation and monetary policy. It determines reservations for retained functions and the permitted uses and conditions for releasing other endowed capacity. The implemented boundary must protect the agreed resources and KSM authority against ordinary unilateral revocation, reassignment or material impairment, including through manager powers, supervision and host upgrades, throughout the term.

The system base uses the planning model for 5 system chains (Asset Hub, Coretime, People, Bridge Hub and Encointer), shared host state and programs, 2 reusable authorizer-program blobs, 1 active and 2 additional solicited Parachain Validation Function (PVF) versions per chain, 3 host-program versions and contingency. The migration allowance provides initial support for 12 other parachains, with 1 PVF version and generic host-side state per chain plus contingency.

KSM governance must define participant quotas, future upgrade backing and permissible reallocation. Participants must secure additional backing before state growth or overlapping upgrades exceed their quotas. Transitional backing may be released only once replacement backing is in place or the dependent state has been removed. The complete footprint entitlement remains protected throughout the agreed term.

KSM governance may permit additional Kusama activity using separately funded JAM coretime where the implemented service supports it. Additional purchases follow their own availability, pricing, duration and protection terms. Their footprint, always-accumulate resources and maintenance needs must fit assessed available capacity or receive additional funded provision. Such purchases do not enlarge this endowment or automatically acquire its resource protections, protected term or stewardship commitments.

**4. Fellowship mandate and duration.** An accepted Polkadot Fellowship mandate covers technical assessment, architecture, implementation, migration and maintenance of the agreed operating scope. The Fellowship should assess the system base and initial migration needs separately and establish a viable arrangement within the reserved compute, assessed footprint and sufficient always-accumulate resources. It should assess use of externally acquired JAM coretime by the Kusama service, including possible direct purchases by users, the associated authorization and operating requirements, and controls over endowed capacity released for use elsewhere.

The mandate preserves Kusama–Polkadot alignment through shared technologies, primitives, standards and interfaces. KSM governance retains material offering decisions. The protected term begins at verified operational availability of the complete package. Material changes to the agreed package, including necessary footprint ceiling revisions, return to the relevant governance bodies.

**5. Funding and implementation.** Payment uses eligible Kusama Vision fund resources under the selected resolution’s amendment of WFC #498 (https://kusama.subsquare.io/referenda/498). Expenditure and outstanding obligations are reconciled, existing commitments are preserved, and the remainder supports the Kusama Vision programme.

This proposed arrangement is subject to acceptance by Web3 Foundation (W3F). W3F may confirm the eligible contribution through an explicit support vote or a conscious neutral abstention explicitly recorded for the Kusama round. Ordinary nonparticipation provides no funding confirmation.

**Effect of this vote.** Approval establishes political acceptance of **one package and one contribution**. Resource allocation, payment and technical changes require authorized implementation actions; the WFC itself transfers no funds and grants no technical privileges.
