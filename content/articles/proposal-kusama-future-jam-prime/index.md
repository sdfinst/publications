# A Proposal for a Kusama Future on JAM Prime

## A position paper on finite resources, strategic choices and Kusama’s next offering

*17 September 2026 by Emiel Sebastiaan and Arief Ernst on behalf of SDFI BV.*

**Status and purpose.** This paper follows *Kusama Futures in the JAM Era*. It proposes a negotiating direction for community discussion, supported by the technical requirements in Appendix A, the economic analysis in Appendix B and a template Wish for Change resolution in Appendix C. Appendix D records community feedback. Funding, implementation and migration require separate approvals from the relevant governance bodies and resource owners.

**Appendix A | technical feasibility.** Appendix A is directed primarily to the **Polkadot Fellowship and JAM implementers** for expert critical assessment of the proposed transition architecture, resource assumptions and sovereignty mechanisms.

**Appendix B | economic feasibility.** Appendix B is directed primarily to **Web3 Foundation** to inform its decisions on **Vision Fund allocations**, with **its economists** invited to critically assess the cost benchmark, financing assumptions and economic case.

**Disclosures and acknowledgements.** The authors write from informed positions. Primary author leads a JAM Implementation project, and his engineering team was the first to fully complete Milestone 1 of the JAM Prize. Additionally, the primary author has business interests in staking operations, in ventures seeking to research and utilise JAM infrastructure, and has economic exposure to both ecosystems. Views expressed are authors’ own and do not represent other JAM implementers or related organisations. Any mandate to pursue or implement the scenarios discussed ultimately rests with KSM DAO and, where Polkadot resources or commitments are involved, potentially with DOT DAO. AI tools were used to assist with research, drafting and editing; the authors take full responsibility and ownership for the arguments, suggestions and conclusions presented. Thanks are due to fellow JAM implementers who provided technical review; such review does not imply endorsement of the paper’s suggestions and conclusions. The authors remain committed to moderating the discussion that follows.

## Abstract

Kusama’s next infrastructure decision will shape what its community can build, how it governs that work and which obligations it must finance. Preserving existing functions, retaining meaningful self-government and developing a distinctive offering must fit within the same resource constraints.

**We propose negotiating 16 total cores for 5 years on JAM Prime, with 450,000 JAMKB-equivalent of footprint, protected KSM control and Fellowship-led migration and maintenance, using US$8.64 million as the complete-package negotiating reference and settling in DOT.**

The proposal combines a finite infrastructure commitment with a renewed vision programme for Kusama’s next era on JAM. Its terms must leave that programme funded. The quantities are planning allocations, the price is a negotiating reference, and the required sovereignty boundary remains to be implemented and verified.

## 1. From the Scenario Analysis to a Transition Hypothesis

### 1.1 The transition hypothesis

The *Scenario Analysis* compared independent operation, economic consolidation and cohabitation. It introduced an endowment as a way to retain a distinct KSM-governed domain while sharing the underlying security infrastructure. This paper develops that possibility into a resource request, vision programme direction and approval process.

**JAM Prime** means the Polkadot-aligned JAM instance contemplated as the successor to Polkadot’s relay chain. **Cohabitation** means shared security infrastructure with separate governance and resource-allocation domains. A **security and resource endowment** provides specified resources and associated control rights for an agreed term for Kusama.

Kusama would adapt Polkadot’s proposed Parachain Service design as a separate **Kusama Parachain Service**, referred to in this document as **host**. Kusama Asset Hub would provide KSM-governed administration, including host upgrades; Kusama Coretime would administer parachain participation and internal allocations. Authorizer programs would enforce admission rules for work submitted to Kusama’s endowed cores. Validator selection would remain at JAM Prime level.

### 1.2 What the technical resource analysis establishes

The **5-chain system baseline** comprises **Asset Hub, Coretime, People, Bridge Hub and Encointer**. The technical reference retains **7 cores** for these chains: 3 for Asset Hub and 1 for each other chain. Rounding to **8 cores** provides allocation flexibility. A 16-core allocation leaves 9 cores beyond the baseline, while 32 cores leave 25.

The wider permissionless ecosystem inventory contains **17 parachains and 41 allocated cores**. Both 16-core and 32-core proposals therefore require explicit treatment of workloads and commitments outside the proposed estate.

The footprint request combines **250,000 JAMKB-equivalent for the 5-chain offering** with **200,000 for initial transition support for 12 other parachains**, for a **450,000 JAMKB-equivalent** planning allocation. It does not establish that all 17 chains can execute within either 16 or 32 cores. Always-accumulate requirements remain to be quantified.

### 1.3 Sovereignty as a delivery condition

The proposed **bounded operational sovereignty** gives KSM governance control over its domain while accepting shared infrastructure, consensus and security. Throughout the term, ordinary unilateral DOT-side actions must be unable to revoke, reassign or materially impair the endowed resources or Kusama’s authority over them. Changes weakening that bundle require valid KSM authorisation, except for conditions expressly agreed and encoded in advance.

Protection must cover assignment and authorisation, footprint backing, always-accumulate configuration, service upgrades, and the manager and supervisory paths capable of overriding them. Resource sufficiency and control must be verified together under one compatible implementation baseline.

## 2. Constraints and Strategic Choices

The endowment must fit both Kusama’s available resources and its purpose. The following positions are the authors’ judgements, open to challenge and revision.

### 2.1 Establish a decision timetable

**Position 1. Kusama needs an infrastructure decision now.** A direction-setting decision should be followed by a timely preparation programme; postponement should identify the information sought, continuing expenditure and point of reconsideration.

### 2.2 Account for independent security

**Position 2. Kusama cannot afford an independent JAM.** A 32-core configuration represents **US$3.456 million annually** at the common benchmark. The authors assess independent operation as imposing a substantially greater financing and engineering burden than a smaller shared-infrastructure package, while accepting that a complete independent-JAM budget and quantified security comparison remain unresolved.

### 2.3 Require a demand case for non-system parachain hosting

**Position 3. A permissionless parachain-hosting proposition is not compelling.** Continued dedicated capacity should require a prepaid commitment covering its allocated package cost and participant-specific costs, or separately approved support for an explicit Kusama purpose. Existing access carries no presumption of indefinitely subsidised renewal.

### 2.4–2.10 Strategic positions

**Position 4.** Securing infrastructure does not give Kusama purpose.  
**Position 5.** Securing infrastructure does not fund its use.  
**Position 6.** The endowment requires a DOT-denominated payment envelope.  
**Position 7.** Capacity and duration compete within a fixed endowment envelope.  
**Position 8.** A finite endowment provides a runway to establish sustainability.  
**Position 9.** KSM needs an explicit role in Kusama’s offering.  
**Position 10.** Every stakeholder group must reconsider its expectations.

The proposed **Future Vision Fund / Programme** would require its own agreed mandate, budget, delegated responsibilities and reporting. A protected infrastructure term and a funded programme are separate commitments.

## 3. The Endowment Package and Negotiating Alternatives

### 3.1 One complete commitment

The requested package is one DOT-denominated commitment for compute, footprint, always-accumulate capacity, enforceable KSM administrative authority, and a Fellowship-led migration and maintenance mandate.

| Component | Proposed requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Compute | **16** identified cores under Kusama’s regime. |
| Footprint | **450,000 JAMKB-equivalent**, or 450,000,000 credits under the planning convention. |
| Accumulation | Protected always-accumulate registration and a quantified allowance sufficient for agreed workloads. |
| Sovereignty | KSM control over internal allocation, authorisation, administration and upgrades, with enforceable restrictions. |
| Migration and maintenance | An accepted Fellowship-led mandate covering the agreed infrastructure and continuing maintenance. |
| Duration | **5 years** from verified operational acceptance. |

### 3.2 The compute benchmark

A **coretime-year (CTY)** is one core allocated for one year. The planning benchmark uses 3 validator-equivalents per core and a US$3,000 monthly operating allowance:

`3 × US$3,000 × 12 months = US$108,000 per CTY`

`Compute benchmark = total cores × years × US$108,000`

The amount is a negotiating reference for the complete package, not a separate derivation of footprint, accumulation, sovereignty, migration or maintenance prices.

### 3.3 Capacity–duration configurations

| Total cores | Scenario A: 80 CTYs / US$8.64m | Scenario B: 60 CTYs / US$6.48m | Scenario C: 40 CTYs / US$4.32m |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 32 | 2.5 years | 1.875 years | 1.25 years |
| 24 | 3⅓ years | 2.5 years | 1⅔ years |
| 16 | 5 years | 3.75 years | 2.5 years |
| 8 | 10 years | 7.5 years | 5 years |

All variants carry the same 450,000 JAMKB-equivalent request. Equal CTYs can entail different obligations because footprint availability, maintenance and protection extend for the full term.

### 3.4–3.6 Financing and DOT-side case

The principal settlement source proposed is an authorised contribution from reconciled Vision Programme DOT resources. The working formula is:

`Potential Vision contribution = 10,000,000 DOT − prior expenditure − outstanding obligations − retained programme funding`

Settlement should use a fixed DOT ceiling and agreed valuation method. Web3 Foundation approval of its contribution, KSM DAO approval of its commitments, and DOT DAO approval of the arrangement remain distinct requirements. DOT DAO’s case depends on identifiable consideration, costs, alternate demand, shared engineering and genuine additional economic activity.

## 4. Suggestions for Kusama’s Strategy and Vision Programme

The endowment should support a focused, KSM-centred economy. The Future Vision Fund / Programme could coordinate the strategy KSM DAO adopts through project selection, requests for proposal, delivery milestones and public reporting. Its mandate must distinguish KSM DAO’s strategic authority, Web3 Foundation’s authority over contributions, and the responsibilities delegated to programme operators.

Potential programme directions require their own specifications, budgets and approvals:

- JAM service architecture and bridgeless interaction with compatible Polkadot services on JAM Prime.
- KSM monetary redesign, including explicit treatment of issuance, obligations, revenue allocation and renewal provisions.
- A cypherpunk digital economy: privacy-preserving KSM, a prospective collateralised `ksmUSD`, and privacy-focused smart contracts.
- A participatory experimental jurisdiction: privacy-preserving governance, personhood and private credentials, and art and social experiments.
- A responsive application hub, including assessment of a 12-core Asset Hub and 500 ms block-production target.

Native JAM services require separate resource, governance, state, code and maintenance assessment. The transition footprint allowance does not automatically cover arbitrary new deployments.

## 5. Proposed Terms, Transition and Approvals

### 5.1 Why 16 cores and 5 years

**We recommend developing Scenario A’s 16-core, 5-year arrangement.** It provides allocation room beyond the retained five-chain baseline and a finite runway for monetary redesign, applications, evaluation and continuation planning. The final offering brief must show how capacity will be used and how the Future Vision Fund / Programme will remain funded.

### 5.2 Continuation timetable

- **Before acceptance:** establish an offering brief, initial priorities, funded programme mandate and treatment of existing obligations.
- **Year one:** implement approved monetary direction and develop funded specifications for differentiated services.
- **Years two and three:** establish use of selected capabilities; by the end of year three choose a financed renewal, extension, resizing or alternative direction.
- **Years four and five:** implement the continuation arrangement.

### 5.3–5.5 Delivery, capacity transition and validators

DOT DAO should be asked to include a Fellowship-led migration and infrastructure-maintenance mandate for the full term, accepted by the Fellowship and delivery teams. The plan must name scope, budgets, reporting, recovery support and continuity arrangements.

The endowment carries no standing commitment to subsidised permissionless non-system hosting. Existing participants require consultation and orderly treatment. At the planning benchmark, one full core represents US$108,000 for one year or US$540,000 for five years, before participant-specific costs.

Validator reductions must be assessed separately. With unchanged issuance and staking rules, fewer validator positions do not change aggregate staking expenditure; changes to aggregate expenditure require a separate decision.

### 5.6–5.7 Governance sequence and proposition

The paper proposes a defined public discussion window before a Wish for Change. The eventual bilateral proposal must return with exact resources and term, fixed DOT ceiling, confirmed funding, accepted delivery responsibilities, reviewed authority and recovery mechanisms, programme budgets, and treatment of existing participants. Approval is required from KSM DAO, DOT DAO and each funding party for its respective commitment.

**Five years to build a Kusama worth renewing.**

## Appendix A: Technical basis and transition path assumptions

### A.1–A.2 Architecture

Kusama could adapt the Polkadot Parachain Service design as a separate Kusama host. Kusama Asset Hub would administer host upgrades, Kusama Coretime would administer participation and internal allocations, and authorizer programs would enforce admission rules. Asset Hub and Coretime are hosted parachains; authorizers are programs.

The bounded endowment must combine identified execution cores, effective footprint backing and adequate always-accumulate capacity. The host holds the `assign` privilege for endowed cores; validator selection and protected service-ID allocation remain at JAM Prime level.

### A.3 Compute reference

| Workload | Parachains | Allocated cores |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Asset Hub | 1 | 3 |
| Coretime | 1 | 1 |
| People | 1 | 1 |
| Bridge Hub | 1 | 1 |
| Encointer | 1 | 1 |
| Other non-system parachains | 12 | 34 |
| **Total** | **17** | **41** |

The retained five-chain continuation reference needs seven cores; eight is the rounded baseline. A 16-core allocation leaves nine cores beyond that baseline. Neither core counts nor footprint allowances establish equivalent JAM performance or an ability to host every present parachain.

### A.4 Footprint reference

The planning convention is **1 JAMKB-equivalent = 1,000 footprint credits**. The five-chain model includes host state and program versions, PVF versions and authorizer programs. It gives a 250,000 JAMKB-equivalent allocation. Initial support for 12 other chains adds 200,000 JAMKB-equivalent, for a combined 450,000 JAMKB-equivalent allocation.

Additional user-funded participation requires effective backing for state, code and upgrades and remains limited by the cores within the endowment.

### A.5 Sovereignty assurance

Bounded operational sovereignty requires that ordinary DOT-side administrative paths cannot revoke, reassign or materially impair the protected bundle during the agreed term. It must protect cores and authorisation, footprint and code availability, an adequate accumulation floor, management and upgrade paths, recovery, activation and expiry.

The manager’s `bless` operation and version-sensitive supervision semantics are design risks. A settlement-enforcing manager or another compatible construction must constrain direct privilege changes, code replacement, succession and supervisory mutation paths. Resource sufficiency and enforceable KSM sovereignty must be verified together under one compatible implementation baseline.

## Appendix B: Economic basis and financing assumptions

The economic model allocates a US$3,000 monthly managed-operation allowance to each validator position. With 1,023 validator positions and 341 cores, that yields three validator-equivalents per core and **US$108,000 per CTY**. The allowance is a planning benchmark; protocol development and returns on bonded security capital are excluded.

| Total cores | 1 year | 5 years | 10 years |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 8 | US$0.864m | US$4.320m | US$8.640m |
| 16 | US$1.728m | US$8.640m | US$17.280m |
| 32 | US$3.456m | US$17.280m | US$34.560m |

The complete US$8.64 million negotiating reference seeks 16 cores for five years together with footprint, accumulation, sovereignty, migration and maintenance. Those additional components are not separately priced by the compute formula and require counterparty agreement.

The original 10 million DOT Vision Programme commitment is not an available balance: expenditure, outstanding obligations and retained programme funding must be reconciled before a ceiling is established. DOT settlement avoids a substantial KSM position for the counterparty, but does not establish counterparty acceptance or a price effect.

## Appendix C: Template KSM DAO Wish for Change proposal

### Seek 16 cores, 450,000 JAMKB-equivalent and 5 years of protected KSM self-government on JAM Prime, with Fellowship-led migration and maintenance

KSM DAO would support developing and negotiating a security-and-resource endowment on JAM Prime on the following basis:

1. Pursue cohabitation as the preferred direction for further planning, subject to a later implementation and migration decision.
2. Seek 16 identified cores, 450,000 JAMKB-equivalent of backed footprint, protected accumulation, a five-year term from verified acceptance, and a US$8.64 million complete-package negotiating reference converted to a fixed DOT ceiling.
3. Require protection from ordinary unilateral DOT-side revocation, reassignment or material impairment, with KSM control over internal allocation, authorisation, administration and upgrades.
4. Seek Fellowship acceptance of a mandate for specification, implementation, validation, migration and full-term maintenance.
5. Require a complete-package settlement specification with valuation method, ceiling, funding, payment protection, activation, non-delivery remedies, recovery and expiry.
6. Reconcile Vision Programme funding, retain programme continuity and require separate authority for Treasury contributions, issuance or participant prepayments.
7. Prepare separate proposals for capacity transition, affected participants and any validator changes.
8. Return complete bilateral terms for KSM DAO approval with DOT-side, Fellowship and funding-party approvals where required.
9. Establish a continuation decision by the end of the third operating year.

Approval would express a strategic and negotiating direction only. It would authorise no transfer, issuance, cancellation of access, validator reduction, implementation expenditure or migration.
